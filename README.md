# HikingApp101 – TrailMind

TrailMind is a personal, offline-first Android hiking app for the UK. It shows **Ordnance Survey maps** (using your own free OS Data Hub key) and **OpenStreetMap** for browsing. It can store licensed maps offline, find waymarked routes or follow imported GPX routes, record a breadcrumb track with a foreground location service, and give you a compass, grid references, elevation profiles and time estimates. In a **trip group**, everyone's phone shows the rest of the party on the map over long-range Bluetooth, with no signal needed, and raises the alarm if someone falls. You can export hikes as GPX for Strava, and prepare briefings for an on-device Gemma model. The layout adapts to the Galaxy Z Fold cover screen and the inner screen.

> **Safety:** TrailMind supports navigation; it does not replace it. Carry a paper map and compass and know how to use them. GPS, batteries and phone signal all fail. In the UK, call **999/112** and ask for **Police → Mountain Rescue**.

## Status

This is a working MVP foundation, not a finished product. Every feature below is implemented against real APIs. Where a feature depends on a third party, the limits are stated honestly, both here and in the app.

If you allowed it, TrailMind sends the text itself and retries every 2 minutes, up to 15 times, until the phone reports it sent. **I'm OK** stops the alarm, and if the SOS already went TrailMind texts the contact that you're OK. On a locked phone **I'm OK** asks for your PIN or fingerprint, so a stranger or a pocket can't cancel the alarm, while anyone can send the SOS text. The Safety page can send the SOS text at any time, and has **emergencySMS** registration and **Text 999** with a prefilled message. | Sending by itself is off by default and needs the SMS permission. TrailMind never texts 999 by itself, even if 999 is your emergency contact: you check that text and send it. Fall detection is a heuristic (freefall, then a 4.5 g impact, then 90 s still) and needs a hike to be recording, so it can miss falls or fire on a dropped phone. With the screen off it relies on the phone delivering motion data between GPS fixes. |

On satellite it keeps to texts and small requests: map tiles, terrain, route snapping, facilities and route search wait for normal signal, and the fall alarm says that calls, including 999 calls, don't work there. | O2 Satellite carries texts, location sharing and the apps O2 supports. TrailMind isn't one of them yet, so expect only texts to get through. It needs a compatible phone (the Z Fold7 is) and O2's satellite bolt-on. The network name is only available with location permission. |
| Guidance | Follow a route to see distance and ascent remaining, time left at your pace, progress, and the next waypoint. **Off-route detection** (with hysteresis) triggers vibration and a notification, and shows the bearing back to the route. You can also "Go to" a point (long-press) for a straight-line bearing and distance. **Retrace my steps** (Safety page, or *Retrace* on the watch) follows your recorded track back to where the hike started. The map and the watch dial mark every kilometre, or mile, along the route. | Guidance runs while the app or recording is active. Off-route alerts with the screen off need a hike to be recording. |
| Daylight guard | Route details and Beacon show when to start to finish before sunset with a chosen 0/30/60 min margin. The map shows start-by guidance until movement begins, then estimated finish, daylight left and a turn-back deadline; alerts start only after on-route movement or recording. Before sunrise and after civil dusk it says it is dark now. The watch receives a short warning over the existing link. | Estimates use planned route pace until enough recorded moving time is available; they exclude breaks and cannot guarantee a safe descent. Sunset is for a level horizon, not surrounding hills. |
| Hike recording | `location` foreground service with GPS every 2 s, filtered for accuracy, jitter and speed. **Every point is written to SQLite immediately.** Pause/resume creates new track segments. It survives the UI being closed, and offers to recover an unfinished hike. Notification actions: Pause and Finish. | Needs "while in use" location only (no background permission). If Android kills the process, recording resumes only when the system restarts the service. Otherwise the app offers recovery. |
| History & export | Each hike shows stats (distance, ascent/descent, moving time, elevation range), a profile and a map view. It can be exported as GPX 1.1 with `<time>` and elevation, using the system save dialog or share sheet. | — |
| Battery-conscious display | Phone and watch heading updates require at least 1° of change and are capped at about 15 per second. Rate-limited changes are delivered when the interval expires, even without another sensor event. Unchanged compass text, map cards, watch styles and map sources are left alone; live GPS and recording stats still update. | Sensor noise above the threshold and genuine GPS or navigation changes still redraw. This is not a battery-life estimate. |
| Strava | Share or save the GPX, then open **strava.com/upload/select**. The in-app guide explains the steps. | No Strava API/OAuth, because that needs a client secret that must not be embedded in an app. See follow-ups. |
| Compass | Rotation-vector sensor (with accelerometer and magnetometer as a fallback), true north via magnetic declination, and bearing to target. Works with the phone flat or upright. Warns when the magnetometer needs calibrating, and when the field it measures does not match the Earth's at your position (strength and dip from the geomagnetic model), which catches interference the sensor itself reports as calibrated. The watch shows the same warnings on its dial. | Phone compasses are easily disturbed by magnets, cases and metal, and a watch by its magnetic charger. |
| Terrain & safety | Elevation profiles, ascent/descent with a 4 m hysteresis, and an OS grid reference (6/8/10-figure) for your position or any long-pressed point. **Terrain heights:** routes without heights (for example an imported GPX) get them from a terrain model automatically on import or with *Add heights*, and a long-press shows the ground height. **Steep ground** (Map layers) shades slopes from 25° (yellow) through 30° and 35° to 40°+ (purple). Terrain tiles are saved with offline map packs. On phones with a **barometer** (the Galaxy Z Fold has one), altitude and recorded ascent come from air pressure anchored to GPS, and a **storm warning** fires while recording if sea-level pressure falls 4 hPa in 3 hours. The Safety page shows pressure and its trend, and lets you copy/share/SMS your location with its grid reference, call 999, and read UK mountain-rescue guidance. | Without a barometer, altitude comes from GPS. It is converted to height above mean sea level on Android 14+ (`AltitudeConverter`); older versions show ellipsoid height, which is ~50 m off in the UK. The pressure trend needs about 2½ hours of readings while the app is open or recording. Terrain comes from Mapzen Terrain Tiles on AWS at about 12 m resolution (Environment Agency data in England, EU-DEM and SRTM elsewhere), so small crags are smoothed; steep ground appears from map zoom 13 and needs signal once per area unless it is in an offline pack. Heights are only added at the route's existing points. Contours are not drawn; full credits are under *Map layers → About sources*. |
| On-device AI | **Beacon**, the offline hike assistant, builds a rule-based **briefing** on the phone. It covers distance, climb, time, whether you'd finish after sunset, waypoints, and the **mountain forecast** at the route's high point for the hour you'd get there: temperature and feels-like, wind and gusts, rain, low cloud and freezing level, with warnings for strong gusts, cold, ice and poor visibility. The forecast comes from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com) (no key needed) and is saved on the phone, so fetch it before you go. You can copy a ready-made **Gemma prompt** and open **Google AI Edge Gallery** (`com.google.aiedge.gallery`) to paste it into AI Chat with a Gemma model you have downloaded. | Edge Gallery has **no public SDK or intent API**, so this is a manual copy → open → paste handoff. TrailMind does not run an LLM itself (see follow-ups). |
| Foldables | Layout depends on window width, not device model. Narrow (<600 dp, e.g. the Fold cover screen) shows the map, full-screen pages and a bottom navigation bar. Wide (≥600 dp, the inner screen or tablets) shows a navigation rail, a side pane and a live map; the **full-screen map** button hides the rail and pane (Back or the same button brings them back). Folding or unfolding rebuilds the layout **without restarting the activity**, so recording, the page stack and the map camera survive. Handles edge-to-edge insets and multi-window. | No hinge or tabletop-posture layouts (that needs the AndroidX WindowManager library). |
| Privacy | No accounts, analytics or ads. Routes, hikes, contacts and the OS key stay on the device, and backup and device transfer are disabled. Map, terrain, weather and route services receive only the area or points they need; texts go only to your chosen contact. *Settings → Privacy* says this in the app, and [docs/privacy-policy.md](docs/privacy-policy.md) is the full policy. | — |
| Watch companion | A Wear OS app (tested for Galaxy Watch4, Wear OS 3+) that works with the phone. Its first page is a **navigation dial**: a compass ring that turns with the watch, a pointer along the route (or back to it when off route, or to a go-to point), and your breadcrumb trail and route drawn heading-up around you, with the bezel setting the scale (100 m to 5 km) and a tap switching to north-up. Other pages: the phone's map (including its offline packs), live hike time/distance/ascent with Start/Pause/Resume/Finish and **Retrace**, and your grid reference, altitude and pressure trend. Off-route and weather alerts vibrate. It uses a Bluetooth RFCOMM link to the watch you pick in *Settings → Watch companion*, with **no Google Play services**. The link keeps running with the phone app in the background or the phone in a pocket (a *Watch link* notification on the phone, an ongoing activity on the watch) and while the watch screen is off. | The phone app must have been opened once to start the link. With no hike recording, the watch closes the link after 15 minutes out of view to save battery, and reconnects when you open TrailMind on it. Starting a hike from the watch while the phone app is closed only works when Android lets TrailMind start location tracking from the background (for example with battery optimisation off for TrailMind); otherwise the watch asks you to open TrailMind on the phone. Position comes from the phone's GPS. No always-on (ambient) map and no Wear tile yet. |

## Architecture

```
core/   Pure Kotlin/JVM, no Android. Fully unit-tested.
  geo/     Geodesy (distance, bearing, cross-track), Web Mercator tiles, OSGB36 National Grid, sunrise/sunset, route-piece joining
  gpx/     Streaming-safe GPX 1.0/1.1 parser (XXE-hardened), GPX 1.1 writer
  stats/   Track statistics (hysteresis ascent, moving time), hike time estimator
  nav/     RouteGuide (snap-to-route, remaining distance/ascent, waypoints), distance marks, off-route detector, GPS track filter
  map/     Tile policy (OSM bulk-download block, template validation), route-corridor tile coverage
  weather/ Barometric altitude and pressure trend, mountain forecast wording and warnings
  ai/      Beacon: offline hike briefing + Gemma prompt builder
  watch/   Phone ↔ watch message format (framing, state, route, track, tiles), shared by both apps
app/    Android app, framework APIs only (no AndroidX / Play Services)
  map/       TileMapView (custom View), TileProvider (memory/disk cache, MBTiles, HTTP), MbTiles, DownloadService
  location/  ForegroundLocation, TrackingService (location FGS), TrackingState, LocationHub
  nav/       NavigationController (route following, off-route alerts)
  data/      SQLite (routes, hikes + points, offline packs), preferences, Waymarked Trails route finder, Open-Meteo forecasts
  ui/        MainActivity (adaptive shell), MapScreen, pages, small UI toolkit
  watch/     WatchLink: Bluetooth RFCOMM server for the watch (state, route, track, map tiles, commands), WatchLinkService
wear/   Wear OS companion, framework APIs only (plus AndroidX core and wear-ongoing): LinkService and PhoneLink (RFCOMM client), WatchMapView, 4 vertical pages
```

The app deliberately depends only on the Android framework plus `:core`. That keeps the dependency surface small and auditable, avoids needing Google Play Services, and let the whole app be compiled and linted here even though Google's Maven repository (AndroidX, MapLibre's AndroidX dependencies, AGP) was unreachable. The trade-off: TrailMind has its own raster tile renderer instead of MapLibre, which is the main follow-up for vector maps. The watch app follows the same rule, apart from AndroidX core and wear-ongoing for the ongoing activity, so it talks to the phone over a plain Bluetooth socket instead of Google's Wearable Data Layer.

## Building

### Android Studio / Gradle (normal route)

Open the folder in Android Studio (JDK 17, SDK 36), then run:

```
./gradlew :app:assembleDebug        # APK in app/build/outputs/apk/debug/
./gradlew :wear:assembleDebug       # watch APK in wear/build/outputs/apk/debug/
./gradlew :core:test                # domain unit tests
./gradlew :app:testDebugUnitTest    # app unit tests (tile licensing rules)
```

GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/android.yml`) runs the unit tests, both debug builds, both minified release bundles and lint on every push to `main` and every pull request, and keeps the debug APKs as artifacts.

### Release builds and Google Play

```
./gradlew :app:bundleRelease :wear:bundleRelease   # app/ and wear/build/outputs/bundle/release/*.aab
```

Release builds are minified with R8 and have unused resources removed. They are signed with your Google Play upload key when `trailmind.upload.storeFile`, `storePassword`, `keyAlias` and `keyPassword` are set in `~/.gradle/gradle.properties` (never in the repo). Otherwise they fall back to the debug key, so they can be installed for testing, and Play rejects them. Both apps take their version from `trailmind.versionCode` and `trailmind.versionName` in `gradle.properties`, and the watch app's version code is 1,000,000 + the phone's, because Play needs a different code for every upload. [docs/google-play.md](docs/google-play.md) walks through the Play Console, with suggested answers for Data safety and the permission declarations, and [docs/privacy-policy.md](docs/privacy-policy.md) is the privacy policy to publish.

### End-to-end test on emulators

```
./scripts/e2e.ps1 -Phone emulator-5554 -Watch emulator-5556
```

It builds and installs both apps, clears TrailMind's data, then drives the UI with simulated GPS: onboarding, GPX import, route guidance, recording, off-route alert, pause/resume, GPX export, fold/unfold, full-screen map, OS maps (including offline enlargement), finding a route online, the mountain forecast, and the watch companion (including the link with the phone app in the background and the watch screen off). It prints PASS/FAIL per check and exits with the number of failures; screenshots and logs go to `out-e2e/`. OS map checks run when `local.properties` (git-ignored) contains `trailmind.osApiKey=<your key>`. Add `-Release` to test the minified release build as a fresh install. Release builds can't be read with `run-as`, so OS tiles are then checked on screenshots, and the two checks that read the tile cache are skipped.

The root `build.gradle.kts` pins **AGP 9.4.1** on the root buildscript classpath (it must share a classloader with the Kotlin plugin), and uses AGP 9's built-in Kotlin support. Kotlin 2.4 needs AGP 9.1 or later: 9.0.x bundles R8 9.0, which can't read Kotlin 2.4 metadata and warns on every class. If the pinned version doesn't resolve, bump it to the latest stable AGP 9.x.

### Installing on a Galaxy Watch4

1. On the watch: *Settings → About watch → Software information*, tap *Software version* five times, then in *Developer options* turn on *ADB debugging* and *Wireless debugging* (called *Debug over Wi-Fi* on older software). The watch and PC must be on the same Wi-Fi.
2. If the watch offers *Pair new device*, run `adb pair <ip>:<pairing port>` with the code it shows. Then run `adb connect <ip>:<port>` with the address it lists.
3. `adb -s <ip>:<port> install -r wear/build/outputs/apk/debug/wear-debug.apk`, and install the phone APK on the phone as usual.
4. On the phone: *Settings → Watch companion*, turn it on, allow *Nearby devices* and pick the watch. Open TrailMind on the watch and allow *Nearby devices* there too.

### Testing trip groups on two phones

Trip groups need real Bluetooth, so test range on two Android 12+ phones; emulators prove the flow but not the radio.

1. Install the same build on both: `adb -s <serial> install -r app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`. A copy installed from Google Play is signed differently, so uninstall it first (this deletes its hikes and settings: export hikes as GPX before).
2. On one phone: *Safety → Trip group → Create group*. On the other: *Join with code*, and type the code the first one shows. Allow *Nearby devices* when asked.
3. Each map now shows the other phone. *Trip group → Bluetooth diagnostics* shows whether the phone has LE Coded (long range), which advertising sets are running and at what power, and how many adverts it hears on each radio.
4. For range, start a hike on both, turn the screens off and walk apart. Then *Share log (CSV)* from *Bluetooth diagnostics*: one row per frame heard, with signal strength, radio (`coded` or `1m`) and the distance between the phones.
5. `adb shell dumpsys bluetooth_manager` lists TrailMind's scanner under `com.trailmind.app` (scan modes and result counts). After 10 minutes of scanning with the screen off, Android lowers the scan rate, so expect updates every 10–30 s until something needs attention.

### Without AGP (what was verified in this repository)

```
./gradlew -Ptrailmind.coreOnly=true :core:test      # Maven Central only
scripts/build-apk-without-agp.sh --lint              # -> out-apk/TrailMind-debug.apk
adb install -r out-apk/TrailMind-debug.apk
```

The script calls aapt2, `kotlinc` 2.4.0 (downloaded from Maven Central with SHA-1 checks into `.toolcache/`), d8, zipalign and apksigner directly, using `-Werror`. It then runs the SDK's standalone Android lint. The debug keystore is generated locally and git-ignored. It reads the version from `gradle.properties`, like the Gradle build. A build-tools d8 older than Kotlin 2.4 warns about Kotlin metadata on every class; nothing in the app uses kotlin-reflect, so those warnings are harmless, and the script counts them in one line (full log in `out-apk/d8.log`).

## Setup in the app

1. **OS maps:** create a free account at [osdatahub.os.uk](https://osdatahub.os.uk/) and a project with the **OS Maps API**. Paste the project API key into *Settings → OS Data Hub key*, tap **Save & test**, and pick *OS Maps – Outdoor* from the layers button.
2. **Offline:** go to *Offline → Download an area*. Choose the visible map area or a route corridor. For Explorer & Landranger, choose *Explorer 1:25k* on a Premium plan to keep zooms 6–9 for up to 24 hours; zooms 0–5 remain in the pack. TrailMind asks before the detail expires; tap *Renew* to keep it another 24 hours, or *Download again* later without choosing the area again. Other OS maps offer Premium zooms 17–19. Alternatively, import a `.mbtiles` file.
3. **Routes:** go to *Routes → Plan* to tap your own route on the map, *Find* to pick a waymarked route, or *Import* to open a `.gpx` file. Then *Follow* it, download a corridor for it, or build a briefing.
4. **Record:** tap **Start hike** on the map. For long hikes, exempt TrailMind from battery optimisation.
5. **Strava:** open the hike, then *Upload to Strava…* → share or save the GPX → upload it at strava.com/upload/select.
6. **Beacon (Gemma):** go to *Beacon*, pick a route, then *Copy prompt & open Edge Gallery* and paste it into AI Chat.
7. **Watch:** see *Installing on a Galaxy Watch4* above. On the watch, swipe up/down between Navigate, Map, Hike and Position. On Navigate and Map, turn the bezel (or use −/+) to zoom; tap the dial for north-up, or the map for a route overview.

## Roadmap

### Off-grid mapping

1. **Explorer-style walking maps offline: done on the phone.** Visible areas and route corridors support 24-hour Premium detail. TrailMind asks before the detail expires and downloads the same area again only when you tap *Renew* or *Download again*. The watch shows OS Outdoor OpenData only, enlarging zoom 16 for closer views; no Premium tiles are sent or persisted on the watch.
2. **Terrain detail: heights and steep-ground overlay done.** z13 Mapzen Terrain Tiles fill missing route heights, provide spot heights and colour slopes from 25° upward. Terrain tiles are saved with downloaded map packs. Contours and hill shading remain future work.
3. **Offline vector maps:** adopt **MapLibre Native** and support vector MBTiles/offline packs. Prefer the newer **OS NGD API – Tiles** for new OS integration because the OS Vector Tile API is expected to retire in autumn 2028.
4. **Detailed OS features:** evaluate the OS Features API and OS NGD data for selectable MasterMap-derived features, paths, access information and other useful trail context. Cache only the data and time periods permitted by the user's licence.
5. **Safer offline preparation: done.** Route details check saved tiles and Explorer expiry along the route. The download card shows the tile count and estimated storage, refuses oversized areas, and Premium downloads state how many detail tiles they will fetch from your OS plan before starting.
6. **Offline route planning:** add on-device walking-route calculation and rerouting over a licensed path network; keep the current imported-GPX and straight-line guidance as offline fallbacks.
7. **Offline place search:** save OS Open Names (OpenData, Open Government Licence) with each offline area, so place and postcode search works with no signal.

### Trip groups next

1. **Two-phone field test (next).**
   - Walk the Fold 7 and S24 Ultra apart on open ground and in woodland, and read the results from the diagnostics log.
   - Measure the reliable range on LE Coded and on 1M, how far apart updates come after Android's 10-minute scan downgrade, and battery use per hour.
   - Tune the radio profiles and the in-app range wording from the results.
2. **Quick messages and meet points.**
   - A third frame type for preset messages ("Waiting at the stile", "Turning back", "Go on without me") and a shared meet-point pin.
   - Each fits in one 30-byte frame and is passed on like a position.
3. **Keep the group together.**
   - A leader or sweeper role, and an alert when the party spreads out beyond a set distance or someone hasn't been heard for a set time.
   - Short trails behind each member's marker, showing which way they're going.
4. **Group roll call over O2 Satellite.**
   - When any member's phone is on satellite, one short text tells the emergency contact about the whole party, for example "4 of 5 OK; Sam last heard 12 min ago at SO 6399 0347".
   - It would use the existing gateway election and group payload.
5. **Find a member.**
   - For the last few hundred metres, a "warmer, colder" search from the Bluetooth signal strength, with vibration, like an avalanche transceiver.
   - On Android 16, the platform `android.ranging` API could add UWB (both test phones have it) or Bluetooth Channel Sounding for distance and direction over the last tens of metres. This still needs testing on the phones.
6. **The group on the watch.** Members' markers on the watch's dial and map, so the party is visible at a glance.
7. **Easier joining.** Show the invite as a QR code (a small encoder in `:core`, no library), or join by holding two phones together over Bluetooth and checking a short code.
8. **Another radio and more phones.**
   - A Wi-Fi Aware experiment, to compare range and battery with LE Coded.
   - A connection-based (GATT) option, so older phones and a future iPhone app can join; iOS can't send the current advertisements.

### Safety and wellbeing

- **Triage after a fall.** The alarm asks three questions, one tap each: injury, can you move, bleeding. The one-byte answer goes in the SOS text and the group's frames, which already have a field for it.
- **Fall detection on the watch.** The wrist sees a fall better than a pocket. The watch would detect it, and the phone would raise the same alarm and SOS.
- **Turn-back advisor.** One line of advice from what TrailMind already knows: daylight left, pace, climb to go, the pressure trend, the forecast and battery.
- **Battery forecast and expedition mode.**
  - Predict the battery left at the finish from how fast it's draining and the time to go.
  - When that's tight, lower the GPS and Bluetooth rates, dim the map and warn early.
- **Spoken alerts.** Read out off-route, daylight, weather and group alerts through earbuds, with the platform's text-to-speech.
- **Check in when signal returns.** If you turn it on, after a long spell without signal TrailMind texts your contact "I'm OK at …" as soon as the phone gets signal.

### Signal and sky

- **Signal memory.** Record where the phone had mobile signal or O2 Satellite along each hike, and show the nearest known signal on the map. A trip group could share it.
- **Sky-view finder.** Satellite texts need open sky. Use the saved terrain heights to estimate how much of the sky is blocked where you stand, and point to a nearby spot with a clearer view.

### Other follow-ups

- Real on-device LLM: bundle **LiteRT-LM / MediaPipe LLM Inference** with a user-supplied Gemma model file, instead of the Edge Gallery handoff.
- Strava API upload via OAuth through **your own backend**, so the client secret stays off the phone.
- Flex mode on the Fold: the platform hinge-angle sensor (`Sensor.TYPE_HINGE_ANGLE`, Android 11) can drive a half-folded layout without AndroidX, with the map on the top half and the controls, compass and group on the bottom.
- Add Robolectric and instrumented UI tests.
