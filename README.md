# HikingApp101 – TrailMind

TrailMind is a personal, offline-first Android hiking app for the UK. It shows **Ordnance Survey maps** (using your own free OS Data Hub key) and **OpenStreetMap** for browsing. It can store licensed maps offline, find waymarked routes or follow imported GPX routes, record a breadcrumb track with a foreground location service, and give you a compass, grid references, elevation profiles and time estimates. In a **trip group**, everyone's phone shows the rest of the party on the map over long-range Bluetooth, with no signal needed, and raises the alarm if someone falls. You can upload hikes to Strava, by hand or automatically, and prepare briefings for an on-device Gemma model. The layout adapts to the Galaxy Z Fold cover screen and the inner screen.

> **Safety:** TrailMind supports navigation; it does not replace it. Carry a paper map and compass and know how to use them. GPS, batteries and phone signal all fail. In the UK, call **999/112** and ask for **Police → Mountain Rescue**.

## Status

### October design and location update

- **Inner-screen unfolding menu:** swipe left across the open menu to fold it away; swipe right from the
  left edge (the visible menu handle avoids Android's Back-gesture area) to unfold it again. You can also
  tap that handle or the existing full-screen button. The menu follows your finger and settles with a
  short slide/fold animation, retaining the current page and scroll position. Map gestures away from
  the edge, vertical menu scrolling, sliders and text selection keep their normal behaviour.
  Android's disabled-animation setting is respected. The cover-screen navigation is unchanged.
  The reopening button is visually scaled to 55% of its original size, with its full tap/swipe target retained.
- **Readable route cards:** status badges sit below names/statistics instead of taking their horizontal space.
  List icons use 40 dp tiles; 16 sp titles can wrap across three lines. The map's active-route name has a
  separate two-line heading below "Following". System font scaling and touch targets are retained.
- **Narrow-menu actions:** paired buttons stack vertically when their full labels cannot fit side by side
  (group creation/invites, route and hike actions, export, settings and map-credit links). Wider panels keep
  them horizontal. Long route/device choices wrap instead of being cut off; normal descriptions still wrap.
- **Routes / Hikes → Select:** choose individual records or **Select all**, then confirm deletion. The followed
  route and recording/paused/unfinished hikes are protected from bulk deletion. Deletion is local: exported
  GPX files, offline maps and copies on Strava are retained. An upload already in flight may finish.
- **Safety → what3words:** explicitly look up the current position. Copy, Share, Text location and emergency
  text preparation include it only while it is fresh (one minute) and the current fix remains in the returned
  square. GPS uncertainty can cover many 3 m squares. Coordinates/grid references remain available without
  network; emergency actions never wait for what3words. The automatic SOS retains its GSM-7/160-character
  limit, omitting the optional words if they cannot fit.
- **More / Compass / Safety → Satellites & telemetry:** a real north-up GNSS sky plot, reported/used counts,
  signal strength and available accuracy, speed, altitude and pressure. Zenith is the centre, horizon the rim.
  No invented satellites; absent or stale reports are labelled. This is not satellite messaging coverage.
- **iPhone preparation:** a [SwiftUI starter](ios/) and [MacinCloud/private TestFlight guide](docs/iphone-development.md)
  are included. This is a foreground-location/Apple Maps preview, **not Android feature parity**. First Xcode
  build, XCTest, signing and physical-iPhone validation are pending access to the Mac.

Android regression coverage for this update includes selection state, heading freshness/quality thresholds,
three-word address validation/expiry, SMS limits and sky projection. The framework-only device runner also checks
real SQLite cascades, protected unfinished hikes, settings persistence, API response parsing and map camera
coordinates beside a pane. Run on a test emulator (replace the serial):

```powershell
.\gradlew.bat :core:test :app:testDebugUnitTest :app:assembleDebug :app:assembleDebugAndroidTest :app:lintDebug
adb -s emulator-5580 install -r .\app\build\outputs\apk\debug\app-debug.apk
adb -s emulator-5580 install -r .\app\build\outputs\apk\androidTest\debug\app-debug-androidTest.apk
adb -s emulator-5580 shell am instrument -w com.trailmind.app.test/com.trailmind.app.RedesignInstrumentation
```

The runner uses a dedicated `redesign-tests-` database/preferences namespace, never the user's library.
It prints `PASS:` on success or `FAIL:` with a stack trace; check that output rather than adb's exit code.
Live what3words account access and outdoor sensor behaviour require separate API-key/device testing.

This is a working MVP foundation, not a finished product. Every feature below is implemented against real APIs. Where a feature depends on a third party, the limits are stated honestly, both here and in the app.

| Auto-pause in a vehicle | If you forget to pause at the car park, TrailMind notices. When the phone keeps moving faster than about 22 km/h (14 mph) for half a minute and covers at least 120 m, it **pauses the hike**, switches the GPS off, leaves the drive out of your track, distance and time, and notifies you (**Resume** or **Finish** from the notification). Fixes during a fast stretch are held back until it's clear whether it was a drive, so a short burst (a bike lent for a minute, a GPS glitch) is saved as normal; short stops at junctions don't end a run. On by default, *Settings → Recording*. | A speed rule, not a sensor that knows you're in a car, so cycling, skiing or a fast train will pause too (turn it off for those). ## Architecture

```
core/   Pure Kotlin/JVM, no Android. Fully unit-tested.
  geo/     Geodesy (distance, bearing, cross-track), Web Mercator tiles, OSGB36 National Grid, sunrise/sunset, route-piece joining
  gpx/     Streaming-safe GPX 1.0/1.1 parser (XXE-hardened), GPX 1.1 writer
  stats/   Track statistics (hysteresis ascent, moving time), hike time estimator
  nav/     RouteGuide (snap-to-route, remaining distance/ascent, waypoints), distance marks, off-route detector, GPS track filter
  map/     Tile policy (OSM bulk-download block, template validation), route-corridor tile coverage, GeoTIFF reader, LiDAR shading, crags and route check
  weather/ Barometric altitude and pressure trend, mountain forecast wording and warnings
  ai/      Beacon: offline hike briefing + Gemma prompt builder
  watch/   Phone ↔ watch message format (framing, state, route, track, tiles), shared by both apps
app/    Android app, framework APIs only (no AndroidX / Play Services)
  map/       TileMapView (custom View), TileProvider (memory/disk cache, MBTiles, HTTP), MbTiles, DownloadService, LidarProvider (EA LiDAR squares)
  location/  ForegroundLocation, TrackingService (location FGS), TrackingState, LocationHub
  nav/       NavigationController (route following, off-route alerts)
  data/      SQLite (routes, hikes + points, offline packs), preferences, Waymarked Trails route finder, Open-Meteo forecasts
  ui/        MainActivity (adaptive shell), MapScreen, pages, small UI toolkit
  watch/     WatchLink: Bluetooth RFCOMM server for the watch (state, route, track, map tiles, commands), WatchLinkService
wear/   Wear OS companion, framework APIs only (plus AndroidX core and wear-ongoing): LinkService and PhoneLink (RFCOMM client), WatchMapView, 4 vertical pages
```

The app deliberately depends only on the Android framework plus `:core`. That keeps the dependency surface small and auditable, avoids needing Google Play Services, and let the whole app be compiled and linted here even though Google's Maven repository (AndroidX, MapLibre's AndroidX dependencies, AGP) was unreachable. The trade-off: TrailMind has its own raster tile renderer instead of MapLibre, which is the main follow-up for vector maps. The watch app follows the same rule, apart from AndroidX core and wear-ongoing for the ongoing activity, so it talks to the phone over a plain Bluetooth socket instead of Google's Wearable Data Layer.

## Building

### Android Studio / Gradle (normal route)

Open the folder in Android Studio (JDK 17, SDK 36), then run:

```
./gradlew :app:assembleDebug        # APK in app/build/outputs/apk/debug/
./gradlew :wear:assembleDebug       # watch APK in wear/build/outputs/apk/debug/
./gradlew :core:test                # domain unit tests
./gradlew :app:testDebugUnitTest    # app unit tests (tile licensing rules)
```

GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/android.yml`) runs the unit tests, both debug builds, both minified release bundles and lint on every push to `main` and every pull request, and keeps the debug APKs as artifacts.

### Release builds and Google Play

```
./gradlew :app:bundleRelease :wear:bundleRelease   # app/ and wear/build/outputs/bundle/release/*.aab
```

Release builds are minified with R8 and have unused resources removed. They are signed with your Google Play upload key when `trailmind.upload.storeFile`, `storePassword`, `keyAlias` and `keyPassword` are set in `~/.gradle/gradle.properties` (never in the repo). Otherwise they fall back to the debug key, so they can be installed for testing, and Play rejects them. Both apps take their version from `trailmind.versionCode` and `trailmind.versionName` in `gradle.properties`, and the watch app's version code is 1,000,000 + the phone's, because Play needs a different code for every upload. [docs/google-play.md](docs/google-play.md) walks through the Play Console, with suggested answers for Data safety and the permission declarations, and [docs/privacy-policy.md](docs/privacy-policy.md) is the privacy policy to publish.

### End-to-end test on emulators

```
./scripts/e2e.ps1 -Phone emulator-5554 -Watch emulator-5556
```

It builds and installs both apps, clears TrailMind's data, then drives the UI with simulated GPS: onboarding, GPX import, route guidance, recording, off-route alert, pause/resume, GPX export, fold/unfold, full-screen map, OS maps (including offline enlargement), finding a route online, the mountain forecast, and the watch companion (including the link with the phone app in the background and the watch screen off). It prints PASS/FAIL per check and exits with the number of failures; screenshots and logs go to `out-e2e/`. OS map checks run when `local.properties` (git-ignored) contains `trailmind.osApiKey=<your key>`. Add `-Release` to test the minified release build as a fresh install. Release builds can't be read with `run-as`, so OS tiles are then checked on screenshots, and the two checks that read the tile cache are skipped.

`./scripts/e2e-features.ps1 [-SkipBuild]` tests auto-pause in a vehicle, group hikes, old maps and Strava on a phone emulator with a **debug** build: a simulated drive (speed included) pauses the hike and leaves the drive out of the distance; a simulated second member (the debug-only `DebugReceiver`) starts, finishes and restarts a group hike, in automatic and ask-first modes; a local stand-in tile server (python with Pillow) takes the place of MapTiler for the key flow, a rejected key, the fade slider and the credit, centred on the Forest of Dean; and `scripts/e2e-mocks/strava_server.py` stands in for Strava's sign-in and upload API for linking through the real browser, a wrong secret, a forged redirect, automatic posting after a hike (token refresh, GPX with times), rate limits, outages, retries, disconnect, and Upload to Strava. It clears TrailMind's data on the emulator.

The root `build.gradle.kts` pins **AGP 9.4.1** on the root buildscript classpath (it must share a classloader with the Kotlin plugin), and uses AGP 9's built-in Kotlin support. Kotlin 2.4 needs AGP 9.1 or later: 9.0.x bundles R8 9.0, which can't read Kotlin 2.4 metadata and warns on every class. If the pinned version doesn't resolve, bump it to the latest stable AGP 9.x.

### Installing on a Galaxy Watch4

1. On the watch: *Settings → About watch → Software information*, tap *Software version* five times, then in *Developer options* turn on *ADB debugging* and *Wireless debugging* (called *Debug over Wi-Fi* on older software). The watch and PC must be on the same Wi-Fi.
2. If the watch offers *Pair new device*, run `adb pair <ip>:<pairing port>` with the code it shows. Then run `adb connect <ip>:<port>` with the address it lists.
3. `adb -s <ip>:<port> install -r wear/build/outputs/apk/debug/wear-debug.apk`, and install the phone APK on the phone as usual.
4. On the phone: *Settings → Watch companion*, turn it on, allow *Nearby devices* and pick the watch. Open TrailMind on the watch and allow *Nearby devices* there too.

### Testing trip groups on two phones

Trip groups need real Bluetooth, so test range on two Android 12+ phones; emulators prove the flow but not the radio.

1. Install the same build on both: `adb -s <serial> install -r app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`. A copy installed from Google Play is signed differently, so uninstall it first (this deletes its hikes and settings: export hikes as GPX before).
2. On one phone: *Safety → Trip group → Create group*. On the other: *Join with code*, and type the code the first one shows. Allow *Nearby devices* when asked.
3. Each map now shows the other phone. *Trip group → Bluetooth diagnostics* shows whether the phone has LE Coded (long range), which advertising sets are running and at what power, and how many adverts it hears on each radio.
4. For range, start a hike on both, turn the screens off and walk apart. Then *Share log (CSV)* from *Bluetooth diagnostics*: one row per frame heard, with signal strength, radio (`coded` or `1m`) and the distance between the phones.
5. `adb shell dumpsys bluetooth_manager` lists TrailMind's scanner under `com.trailmind.app` (scan modes and result counts). After 10 minutes of scanning with the screen off, Android lowers the scan rate, so expect updates every 10–30 s until something needs attention.

On an emulator, a **debug build** has a stand-in for the radio, so group hikes (and a member on the map) can be tried with no second phone. It seals real adverts under the group's keys and feeds them to the same code the radio does:

```
adb shell am broadcast -n com.trailmind.app/.debug.DebugReceiver -a com.trailmind.app.debug.GROUP_CREATE
adb shell am broadcast -n com.trailmind.app/.debug.DebugReceiver -a com.trailmind.app.debug.GROUP_PEER --ei id 20481 --es name Alex --ei flags 5
adb shell am broadcast -n com.trailmind.app/.debug.DebugReceiver -a com.trailmind.app.debug.GROUP_STATE
```

`flags` is the member's frame flags (1 recording, 2 paused, 4 group hike), so `5` is "Alex started a group hike" and `0` is "Alex finished". `GROUP_STATE` logs what the phone sees under the tag `TrailMindDebug`. The receiver, and a switch to a local old-map tile server (`OLD_MAPS_URL`), exist only in the debug source set (`app/src/debug`), never in release builds.

### Without AGP (what was verified in this repository)

```
./gradlew -Ptrailmind.coreOnly=true :core:test      # Maven Central only
scripts/build-apk-without-agp.sh --lint              # -> out-apk/TrailMind-debug.apk
adb install -r out-apk/TrailMind-debug.apk
```

The script calls aapt2, `kotlinc` 2.4.0 (downloaded from Maven Central with SHA-1 checks into `.toolcache/`), d8, zipalign and apksigner directly, using `-Werror`. It then runs the SDK's standalone Android lint. The debug keystore is generated locally and git-ignored. It reads the version from `gradle.properties`, like the Gradle build. A build-tools d8 older than Kotlin 2.4 warns about Kotlin metadata on every class; nothing in the app uses kotlin-reflect, so those warnings are harmless, and the script counts them in one line (full log in `out-apk/d8.log`).

## Setup in the app

1. **OS maps:** create a free account at [osdatahub.os.uk](https://osdatahub.os.uk/) and a project with the **OS Maps API**. Paste the project API key into *Settings → OS Data Hub key*, tap **Save & test**, and pick *OS Maps – Outdoor* from the layers button.
2. **Offline:** go to *Offline → Download an area*. Choose the visible map area or a route corridor. For Explorer & Landranger, choose *Explorer 1:25k* on a Premium plan to keep zooms 6–9 for up to 24 hours; zooms 0–5 remain in the pack. TrailMind asks before the detail expires; tap *Renew* to keep it another 24 hours, or *Download again* later without choosing the area again. Other OS maps offer Premium zooms 17–19. Alternatively, import a `.mbtiles` file.
3. **Routes:** go to *Routes → Plan* to tap your own route on the map, *Find* to pick a waymarked route, or *Import* to open a `.gpx` file. Then *Follow* it, download a corridor for it, or build a briefing.
4. **Record:** tap **Start hike** on the map. For long hikes, exempt TrailMind from battery optimisation. In a trip group, the others' phones record their own copy too (*Trip group → Group hikes*), and if you forget to pause at the car park, TrailMind pauses for you (*Settings → Recording*).
5. **Old maps:** create a free account at [maptiler.com](https://cloud.maptiler.com/account/keys/) and paste a key into *Settings → Old maps (MapTiler key)*, then **Save & test**. Open *Map layers → Old maps* and tap **Forest of Dean**, or open a route and tap **See it on an old map**. The slider fades the old map over today's.
6. **Strava:** open any hike and tap **Upload to Strava**: the GPX is saved to Downloads and Strava's upload page opens, where you tap *Choose file*, pick it and *Save*. For automatic posting (needs a Strava subscription) go to *Settings → Set up Strava*: create an application at [strava.com/settings/api](https://www.strava.com/settings/api) with **Authorization Callback Domain** `localhost`, paste its Client ID and Client Secret into TrailMind, tap **Connect with Strava** and approve. Never paste the secret anywhere but TrailMind.
7. **Beacon (Gemma):** go to *Beacon*, pick a route, then *Copy prompt & open Edge Gallery* and paste it into AI Chat.
8. **Watch:** see *Installing on a Galaxy Watch4* above. On the watch, swipe up/down between Navigate, Map, Hike and Position. On Navigate and Map, turn the bezel (or use −/+) to zoom; tap the dial for north-up, or the map for a route overview.
9. **SOS:** set an emergency contact in *Settings*. On the *Safety* page, choose whether TrailMind may send the SOS text itself (*Send it without asking me*), and register for emergencySMS before you go (*Register* opens a text of "register" to 999).

## Roadmap

Facts about third-party services and devices below were checked in September 2026.

### Off-grid mapping

1. **Explorer-style walking maps offline: done on the phone.** Visible areas and route corridors support 24-hour Premium detail. TrailMind asks before the detail expires and downloads the same area again only when you tap *Renew* or *Download again*. The watch shows OS Outdoor OpenData only, enlarging zoom 16 for closer views; no Premium tiles are sent or persisted on the watch.
2. **Terrain detail: heights and steep-ground overlay done.** z13 Mapzen Terrain Tiles fill missing route heights, provide spot heights and colour slopes from 25° upward. Terrain tiles are saved with downloaded map packs. Contours, and hill shading outside England (see Planning item 4), remain future work.
3. **Offline vector maps:** adopt **MapLibre Native** and support vector MBTiles/offline packs. Prefer the newer **OS NGD API – Tiles** for new OS integration because the OS Vector Tile API is expected to retire in autumn 2028.
4. **Detailed OS features:** evaluate the OS Features API and OS NGD data for selectable MasterMap-derived features, paths, access information and other useful trail context. Cache only the data and time periods permitted by the user's licence.
5. **Safer offline preparation: done.** Route details check saved tiles and Explorer expiry along the route. The download card shows the tile count and estimated storage, refuses oversized areas, and Premium downloads state how many detail tiles they will fetch from your OS plan before starting.
6. **Offline route planning:** snap and reroute through the **BRouter** Android app's local routing service, as OsmAnd and Locus Map do, using its UK segments on the phone. That works without signal and takes load off the community server. Later, build routing into TrailMind over a licensed path network. Keep the current imported-GPX and straight-line guidance as offline fallbacks.
7. **Offline place search:** save OS Open Names (OpenData, Open Government Licence) with each offline area, so place and postcode search works with no signal.

### Trip groups next

1. **Two-phone field test (next).**
   - Walk the Fold 7 and S24 Ultra apart on open ground and in woodland, and read the results from the diagnostics log.
   - Measure the reliable range on LE Coded and on 1M, how far apart updates come after Android's 10-minute scan downgrade, and battery use per hour.
   - Tune the radio profiles and the in-app range wording from the results.
2. **Quick messages and meet points.**
   - A third frame type for preset messages ("Waiting at the stile", "Turning back", "Go on without me") and a shared meet-point pin.
   - Each fits in one 30-byte frame and is passed on like a position.
3. **Keep the group together.**
   - A leader or sweeper role, and an alert when the party spreads out beyond a set distance or someone hasn't been heard for a set time.
   - Short trails behind each member's marker, showing which way they're going.
4. **Group roll call over O2 Satellite.**
   - When any member's phone is on satellite, one short text tells the emergency contact about the whole party, for example "4 of 5 OK; Sam last heard 12 min ago at SO 6399 0347".
   - It would use the existing gateway election and group payload.
5. **Find a member.**
   - UWB distance on Android 16: done (see *Finding a member (UWB)*).
   - For the last few hundred metres, and phones without UWB, a "warmer, colder" search from the Bluetooth signal strength, with vibration, like an avalanche transceiver.
   - Direction by UWB needs phones whose Ranging module reports angles, which neither test phone does. Bluetooth Channel Sounding, through the same `android.ranging` API, needs Bluetooth 6.0 on both phones.
6. **The group on the watch.** Members' markers on the watch's dial and map, so the party is visible at a glance.
7. **Easier joining.** Show the invite as a QR code (a small encoder in `:core`, no library), or join by holding two phones together over Bluetooth and checking a short code.
8. **Another radio and more phones.**
   - A Wi-Fi Aware experiment, to compare range and battery with LE Coded.
   - A connection-based (GATT) option, so older phones and a future iPhone app can join; iOS can't send the current advertisements.

### Safety

Items 1–3 come first. They are small and can be tested with one phone and one watch.

1. **Distress beacon:** the International Mountain Distress Signal, six whistle blasts and six torch flashes in a minute and then a minute's pause, with a screen strobe and the watch joining in. It starts by hand, or by itself once an SOS text has gone, so rescuers can find you in the dark or in cloud without signal.
2. **Signal memory:** record the phone's service (own network, emergency calls only, none, satellite) with each track point. Show where you last had signal ("0.8 km back, 60 m lower") with a *Go to* button, say when 999 calls can still connect through another network, and add a map layer of where you had signal on past hikes.
3. **Battery forecast:** measure the drain while recording and compare it with the time left on the route ("15% at 16:40, 50 min before you finish"). Then offer a low-power mode with slower GPS, no downloads and a black cover-screen view.
4. **Fall alarm on the watch:** the Watch4 detects falls too and runs the countdown on the wrist. Using both devices cuts false alarms from a dropped phone and catches falls that a phone in a pack misses.
5. **Triage questions:** after a fall or an SOS, three taps (injury, mobility, bleeding) fill the existing 1-byte triage code. A decision tree is enough, so no on-device model is needed.
6. **Rescue mode:** after an SOS, or by hand, TrailMind concentrates on keeping you findable. A casualty card gives what 999 and mountain rescue ask for, in that order: OS grid reference, latitude and longitude, height, GPS accuracy, time, battery, party size and the triage answers. It leads with the grid reference, not what3words, which rescue teams have reported mix-ups with. GPS slows and the screen dims to save battery, and TrailMind asks you to keep the phone out of flight mode, because airborne phone locators such as Lifeseeker can only find a phone with no coverage while its radio is on.
7. **GNSS health:** read the raw GNSS measurements the Fold7 reports (automatic gain control, signal strength, multipath flags, and agreement between the L1 and L5 bands) to say when the position is doubtful, jammed or spoofed, and hold off-route alerts until it recovers. Android has no jamming API, but gain control falls sharply under strong jamming, and published work detects both jamming and spoofing on Android phones.
8. **Dead reckoning:** when GNSS drops out in a gorge, in dense forest or under jamming, carry the track on from steps, the compass and the barometer, so *Retrace* still works. The estimate drifts with distance, so the map shows it with a growing circle and hands back to GNSS as soon as a good fix returns.
9. **Hike as a Live Update:** show the recording as an Android 16 Live Update (`Notification.ProgressStyle`) on the lock screen and in the status bar, with waypoints as points along its bar. On Android 17 its text can use the new semantic colours: orange off route, red for the fall alarm and storm warning.
10. **Turn-back advisor:** one line of advice from what TrailMind already knows: daylight left, pace, climb to go, the pressure trend, the forecast and battery.
11. **Spoken alerts:** read out off-route, daylight, weather and group alerts through earbuds, with the platform's text-to-speech.
12. **Check in when signal returns:** if you turn it on, after a long spell without signal TrailMind texts your contact "I'm OK at …" as soon as the phone gets signal.

### Frontier: group mesh and satellite

1. **Group finder: done as trip groups** (see *Trip groups next*). Encrypted heartbeats in long-range Bluetooth LE adverts (LE Coded PHY), relayed by the existing three-hop routing, show group members on the map without signal. Close in, Android 16's `RangingManager` gives the distance by UWB (done, see *Finding a member (UWB)*); direction needs phones that report angles, and Wi-Fi Aware RTT isn't used yet. It uses the framework's Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Aware APIs, not Nearby Connections, which needs Google Play services. A shared group code stops strangers reading or tracking the adverts. The Fold7 has LE Coded PHY, Wi-Fi Aware and UWB, but not Bluetooth 6.0 Channel Sounding (it has Bluetooth 5.4). UWB was tested with a Galaxy S24 Ultra; Bluetooth range still needs the two-phone field test.
2. **Satellite check-ins without a server:** on O2 Satellite or without signal, text the emergency contact a grid reference every few hours, with a daily cap and a link to a static web page. The group data travels in the link after `#`, which browsers never send to a server, and the page decodes it on the contact's phone. A bit-packed record of about 8 bytes, with positions relative to the sender, fits all eight hikers in one text. Today's protobuf codec fits about five in 256 bytes. Testing needs an O2 SIM with the satellite bolt-on, and Google Play may not accept `SEND_SMS` for routine check-ins.
3. **O2 Satellite data:** [O2 Satellite](https://news.virginmediao2.co.uk/virgin-media-o2-becomes-the-first-uk-mobile-network-to-switch-on-direct-to-device-satellite-connectivity-with-the-launch-of-o2-satellite/) has carried texts and data for listed apps since 26 February 2026, over Starlink Direct to Cell, as a £3-a-month bolt-on. AllTrails, Google Maps and WhatsApp were among the launch apps, and O2's help page has a section for app developers. Getting TrailMind listed would let its existing satellite data path send check-ins and fetch the mountain forecast beyond phone signal. VodafoneThree has Ofcom approval and customer trials for a satellite service with AST SpaceMobile, so Three-network SIMs such as SMARTY may follow.
4. **Meshtastic bridge:** send your position and short texts through your own [Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org) LoRa radio, which relays over kilometres of hillside, far beyond Bluetooth, and show other group members' radios on the map. TrailMind would talk to the radio through Meshtastic's documented Bluetooth client API (one GATT service with *ToRadio*, *FromRadio* and *FromNum*), with no Play services or server. Meshtastic's protobuf definitions are GPL-3.0, so check how that affects TrailMind before shipping.
5. **Not planned from the Frontier spec:**
   - A cloud backend, and rescue dispatch through it. It would break the no-server privacy promise and hold group members' locations, and UK rescue is reached through 999, which TrailMind already prefills.
   - AR satellite aiming. Starlink direct-to-cell hands over between hundreds of low satellites, so open sky is what matters (see *Real horizon from terrain*). ARCore also needs Play services.
   - An on-device model for triage (see Safety item 5).
   - Galileo HAS corrections and Galileo emergency warnings. HAS needs the E6 signal, which phones don't receive, or an internet correction stream. The warning service (EWSS) is still in development, with no phone reception announced.

### Planning and forecasts

1. **Real horizon from terrain:** work out the skyline from the offline terrain tiles. That gives sunset behind the hills instead of over a level horizon, an open-sky hint for satellite texts ("clear sky 200 m NE, 30 m higher"), and ice warnings for sections that stay in shade all day below freezing.
2. **Summit forecast:** take the wind at the high point's height from Open-Meteo's pressure-level winds, because the 10 m wind reads low on summits, and add thunderstorm risk from weather codes 95–99 and CAPE.
3. **Hill bagging:** bundle the [Database of British and Irish Hills](https://www.hill-bagging.co.uk/dobih) (CC BY 4.0, 20,930 hills including Munros, Wainwrights, Marilyns and Nuttalls), credited with its version and a link as its authors ask. Tick off summits from recorded hikes and search hills offline.
4. **1 m LiDAR terrain (England): done.** The Environment Agency's [LIDAR Composite DTM](https://environment.data.gov.uk/dataset/13787b9a-26a4-4775-8523-806d13af58fc) (1 m, about 99% of England, OGL) comes over its WCS one 256 m square at a time. It shows as 1 m hill shading with crags, saves along a route with its offline map, and the route's *Terrain check* lists stretches steep enough for hands and crags near the line. Elsewhere TrailMind keeps the ~12 m model. Scotland and Wales publish their own LiDAR, but coverage and access still need checking.
5. **Exposure grade:** grade each section of a route by its slope, nearby drop-offs and where a slip would run out, adapting the automated avalanche-terrain classification AutoATES v2.0 to summer steep ground, on the LiDAR where it exists.
6. **River crossings:** flag fords and stepping stones on a route and show the nearest upstream Environment Agency gauge's level against its typical range, its trend and any flood warnings, from the free [flood-monitoring API](https://environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring/doc/reference) (OGL, no key). The EA says the API should not be relied on for safety-critical use, and outside floods readings may arrive only once or twice a day, so TrailMind shows each reading's age and treats it as advice. SEPA and NRW publish river levels for Scotland and Wales, but their APIs still need checking.
7. **Skyline check:** draw the terrain's skyline over the camera view to name hills and confirm which way you face. Fixing your position without GPS by matching the camera's skyline to the terrain has been shown in research in hilly areas, but its accuracy on a phone is unproven, so it would start as a cross-check.
8. **Later, needing outside data:** tide windows for coastal crossings (Admiralty API, with your own key), last buses for linear walks, access land with its temporary restrictions and fire-risk closures, and wild-camping rules.

### Other follow-ups

- **On-device AI with Gemma 4:** bundle [LiteRT-LM](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/LiteRT-LM), which needs no Play services, and let the user download [Gemma 4](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core) E2B or E4B (about 1 to 2.5 GB). Gemma 4 takes speech and photos directly and can call functions, so Beacon could answer spoken questions with gloves on by calling TrailMind's own route, terrain, daylight and forecast code, read photos of trail signs and paper maps, and turn a spoken account into a short follow-up text. Plant and fungus photos would get candidate names and risks, never a verdict that something is safe to eat. Gemini Nano isn't an option because it runs through Google's AICore service. This replaces the Edge Gallery handoff.
- **Strava, for a public release:** automatic posting works for one person (and up to nine others) through their own Strava application, which needs each of them to have a subscription. A listing for everyone would need Strava's review for a higher tier, and a small backend to hold the client secret. Strava is also moving the API to `https://www.api-v3.strava.com` (from 4 January 2027) and retiring `oauth/deauthorize` for `oauth/revoke` (1 June 2027); TrailMind uses the old addresses today, so both need updating by then.
- **Tabletop mode on the Fold:** when the phone is half open, show the map on the top half and the compass, grid reference and SOS on the bottom. The Fold7 reports its hinge angle through the framework sensor API, so AndroidX WindowManager isn't needed.
- Add Robolectric and instrumented UI tests.
